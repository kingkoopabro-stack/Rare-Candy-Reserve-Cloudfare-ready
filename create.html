3D Model Rendering

Learn to build and render 3D models with free tools like Blender — characters, props, and scenes for games, prints, and product shots. 3D artists work remotely for games, film, and online shops, and printed models sell from home.

Free software

  • Blender — free download — the full 3D studio: modelling, sculpting, animation, rendering. Free and open source, no account needed.
  • Tinkercad — free, in the browser — free browser-based 3D design from Autodesk — no install, drag-and-drop shapes; the easiest first step into 3D, and it exports STL files for 3D printing.
  • PrusaSlicer — free download — free slicer that turns a 3D model into G-code a 3D printer can print — works with almost any printer, not just Prusa.
  • Blockbench — free, in the browser or as an app — free 3D model editor built for low-poly / blocky models (Minecraft-style, game assets) — runs in the browser or as a desktop app.

Paid / shareware tools — when free isn't enough

  • Autodesk Maya — paid, 30-day free trial — the film and games industry standard for modelling, animation and rigging. $255/month or $2,010/year; Maya Indie is $330/year for artists earning under $100k; free for students and educators. Learn it when you want studio work.
  • Maxon ZBrush — paid, 14-day free trial — digital sculpting: push, pull and carve a model like clay, the tool behind most game and film characters. $49/month or $399/year; the iPad version has a free base version to try first.
  • Adobe Substance 3D Painter — paid, 30-day free trial — paint textures straight onto a 3D model — rust, scratches, fabric — so it looks real instead of flat plastic. Texturing plan $24.99/month ($20.82/month billed annually); also sold on Steam as a $199.99 one-time purchase.

Free vs paid, plainly: Blender does the whole job for free, and most beginners never need anything else. Maya is what big studios hire for, ZBrush is for sculpting organic shapes like faces and creatures, and Substance Painter is for textures — pay for one only when a specific job or project needs what it does best.

Writing

Learn to write stories, scripts, articles, and copy that people actually want to read — with practice and feedback right here. Writers work from anywhere: freelance articles, scripts, books, and web copy, all from home.

Free software

  • LibreOffice Writer — free download — a full word processor for stories, scripts, and books. Free and open source, opens Word files too.
  • yWriter — free download — free novel-writing app that breaks a book into chapters and scenes.
  • Manuskript — free download — free, open-source writing/planning tool with outliner, characters, and plots.
  • Google Docs — free, in the browser — free word processor in the browser with autosave and easy sharing — needs a free Google account.
  • Wattpad — free to join — free place to publish and share stories and get reader feedback.

Paid writing tools — worth it when you write every day

  • Scrivener — paid, $59.99 one-time, 30-day free trial — the standard novel-writing app: chapters, scenes, research notes and character sheets all in one project, with a corkboard to shuffle scenes around. One-time purchase per platform (Mac or Windows), $23.99 on iOS; the trial only counts days you actually use it.
  • Ulysses — paid, $39.99/year or $5.99/month, free trial — a clean, distraction-free writing app for Mac, iPad and iPhone that keeps every draft in one library and exports straight to ebooks, PDFs and Word. Apple only; a $199.99 perpetual license is also sold on the App Store.

Free vs paid, plainly: LibreOffice or Google Docs plus yWriter will carry a whole novel for $0. Scrivener is worth the one-time $59.99 when you're juggling chapters, research and rewrites every day — it's organisation, not magic words. Ulysses is the same idea for Apple users who want the cleanest possible screen to write on.

  • USDA Plant Hardiness Zone Map — look up your zone by ZIP code. North Florida is mostly zones 8a–9a. Your zone tells you which plants survive your winters — not when to plant them.
  • Planting calendar for Starke, FL (Old Farmer's Almanac) — your own dates, by ZIP code: when to start seeds indoors, transplant out, and plant your last crop. For Starke in 2026 the Almanac puts the last spring frost around Mar 1 and the first fall frost around Dec 10 — check the link for the current year.

Paid apps — a plant helper in your pocket

  • Planta — free to download, Premium around $35.99/year — tells you exactly when to water, fertilize, mist and repot each plant, based on your plant and your home. Includes a light meter for finding the right spot, photo ID, and Dr. Planta, which diagnoses sick plants and suggests a fix. A 14-day free trial of Premium is usually offered — prices vary with promos, so check in the app.
  • PictureThis — free to download, Pro around $30/year — snap a photo and it identifies the plant in seconds, with care guides, toxicity warnings for pets and kids, and an AI plant doctor for sick plants. The fastest ID of the apps here, and the one to reach for when you don't know what a plant is.

Free vs paid, plainly: the UF/IFAS guides and the Almanac calendar above are the real knowledge, and they're free — an app can't replace knowing your seasons. Planta is worth paying for when you're keeping a lot of houseplants alive and want reminders that adjust to each one; PictureThis is worth it when you keep meeting plants you can't name. Neither one is a substitute for the free Extension guides when you're deciding what to plant in the ground.

Mycology

Learn to grow mushrooms — identification, sterile technique, and cultivation — safely and step by step. Mycology turns into gourmet and medicinal mushroom sales, kits, and cultures, all from home.

Free tools + know-how

  • FreshCap Mushrooms — Grow Mushrooms Easy in a 5-Gallon Bucket — the easiest first grow: a bucket, aspen wood chips, and oyster mushroom spawn. The video is on the page. Start here.
  • FreshCap Mushrooms — The 3 Levels of Mushroom Growing — level 1: start from a kit. Level 2: start from spawn. Level 3: start from culture. Move up one level at a time.

  • Cornell Small Farms — Mushrooms (Guide to Urban Farming) — a university extension guide: easiest species for home and sale (oyster, shiitake, wine cap), what each grows on, indoor vs. outdoor methods. Free, no paywall.

  • North Spore Spray & Grow kits — from $29.99 — a ready-to-fruit block you mist with water: blue oyster, pink oyster, lion's mane and shiitake blocks are $34.99, golden oyster $29.99. Free vs paid, plainly: a $30–$35 kit is the cheapest way to learn whether you actually enjoy growing — it fruits in two weeks with almost no setup. The bucket and straw methods in the free guides above cost less per harvest once you know what you're doing, so start with a kit, then graduate to bulk grows when you're ready to sell.

Substrate + sterilization basics — don't guess this part

MaterialTreatmentTemp / time
Grain spawn (rye, oats, millet)Full sterilize in a pressure cooker121°C / 250°F at 15 PSI — 90 min for quart jars, 2.5–3 hrs for big bags
Supplemented sawdust blocksFull sterilizeSame 121°C / 15 PSI — 2.5–3 hrs
Straw / coco coir (oysters)Pasteurize — hot-water soak, no pressure cooker needed60–65°C (140–149°F) for 4–6 hrs, or a 65–85°C soak overnight (FreshCap bucket method)
Oyster bucket growLayer spawn + pasteurized aspen chips, keep cool + darkFully colonized in 10–21 days; fruiting needs humidity around 80–100%

Sterilization kills everything — that's what grain and rich substrates need, because any survivor outruns your mycelium. Pasteurization only knocks the competition back, which is enough for straw and coir with aggressive species like oysters. Times depend on load size — check the guides linked above for your exact setup. A regular Instant Pot-style cooker only reaches about 10–12 PSI (~240°F), not the 15 PSI / 250°F target — a stovetop pressure canner is the right tool for grain.

Safety, plain and simple: never eat a wild mushroom based on an app, a photo, or a guess. Get a local expert to confirm it in person, every time. This tab is about growing gourmet mushrooms at home — oysters, shiitake, lion's mane — not eating what you found in the woods.

What it leads to: gourmet mushroom sales, grow kits, cultures, and spawn — all from home, with a small footprint. Oysters are the beginner species: fast, forgiving, and they outcompete most contamination. Start with a kit or a bucket, sell your first harvest, then scale.

Ayurvedic / Alternative Medicine

Learn the basics of Ayurvedic and alternative medicine — herbs, routines, and traditional approaches to feeling better. This is learning for life and for helping others, including wellness and herbal work from home.

Start with what the science actually says

  • Ayurvedic Medicine: In Depth (NCCIH — National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health) — what Ayurveda is, what the studies do and don't show, and the safety warnings: some Ayurvedic preparations have been found to contain lead, mercury, or arsenic in amounts that can be toxic.
  • NCCIH — Herbs at a Glance — short, evidence-based fact sheets on individual herbs (ashwagandha, turmeric, and more) — each covering safety and interactions.
  • Banyan Botanicals — Ayurvedic Health Guides — free long-form learning guides on digestion, sleep, stress, the doshas, and seasonal routines.
  • Coursera — Herbal Medicine (University of Minnesota) — free university course on herbal medicine — history, safety, quality control, and herb-drug interactions. Free to audit; the certificate is paid.

Paid courses — learning from a teacher, not just a page

  • Kerala Ayurveda Academy — Principles of Ayurveda (live online), $195 — a two-day live online course (17 hours, taught by Vaidya Jayarajan Kodikannath) covering the foundations: doshas, digestion, daily routine and the classical texts. No prerequisites, materials included, and it's the prerequisite for the Academy's longer workshops. Full professional programs run into the thousands, so this short course is the sensible first step if you want a real teacher.

Free vs paid, plainly: the free NCCIH pages and Banyan guides above teach the ideas for $0 — start there and read the safety warnings first. A $195 live course is worth it when you want to ask questions and learn the system properly from a teacher, not when you're just curious. Be careful with any school promising a professional credential: since July 1, 2026, only graduates of AAC-accredited or candidate schools can sit NAMA certification exams — Kerala Ayurveda Academy is an AAC candidate.

Clothing line

Learn to design, print, and sell your own clothing line — from the first sketch to a finished shirt people want to wear. Print-on-demand means you can run a clothing brand from home with no stock.

Free to start — print on demand

  • Printful — print on demand — free to start, no order minimums: you design, a customer orders, Printful prints and ships it. You only pay when an order is placed. Free Design Maker for mockups.
  • Printify — free plan — free plan with no monthly fee, up to 5 stores, unlimited designs. You pay the product + shipping cost per order. The paid Premium plan ($39/month) mainly buys product discounts.
  • Fourthwall — free storefront — free storefront + merch platform, no monthly fee and no platform fee on physical sales except payment processing; handles support and taxes. Chris's chosen merch platform for the shop.
  • Bonfire — free, campaign-based — free campaign-based selling — launch a design, buyers order, Bonfire prints and ships; you keep the profit above the base cost. Per-tee profit runs lower than Fourthwall — check base costs before you launch.

Paid design tools — making art worth printing

  • Procreate — $12.99 one-time (iPad) — the drawing app most merch artists actually use: hundreds of handmade brushes, layers, and a canvas up to 16k wide. One-time purchase, no subscription, free updates for life. iPad only (Procreate Pocket for iPhone is $5.99). The Graphic design tab has more free drawing tools if you're on a computer.
  • Canva Pro — $144/year or $18/month, one person — the fast way to turn an idea into a print-ready design: 3.6M+ templates, 141M+ premium assets, background remover, one-click resize, and Brand Kits to keep your colors and fonts consistent. The free plan is genuinely useful — Pro is for when you're selling every week and want the premium library and the background remover.
  • Adobe Illustrator — paid, $22.99/month (annual plan, billed monthly), 7-day free trial — the industry standard for vector art: logos and graphics that stay sharp at any size, which is exactly what print shops want. Worth it when your designs are selling and you need print-ready files; until then, the free tools in the Graphic design tab do the job.

Free vs paid, plainly: GIMP and Krita (Graphic design tab) plus a free Printful or Fourthwall shop will launch a clothing line for $0 — start there and spend nothing until a design sells. Procreate's one-time $12.99 is the best first purchase for anyone drawing on an iPad; Canva Pro earns its $144/year when you're making new designs every week; Illustrator is for when print shops start asking for vector files by name.

The honest picture

ProsCons
  • Print-on-demand means no inventory and no upfront stock cost — you pay only after a sale.
  • Your designs are the product — a strong design + audience beats a big budget.
  • Fourthwall and Bonfire give you a real storefront free, with fulfilment, support and tax handled.
  • Thin per-item profit — base print + shipping costs take most of a $28 tee’s price on the cheaper platforms.
  • You don’t control print quality or shipping speed, and samples cost real money.
  • Standing out is the hard part — thousands of POD shops sell similar shirts, so marketing is the actual job.

How to start: design 3–5 pieces for one clear audience, put them on a free Fourthwall shop (no upfront cost), order a sample of your best seller to check print quality and fit, photograph the real thing, and launch with that small drop — add designs only once the first ones are selling.

Design your art in the Graphic design tab (GIMP or Krita — both free), then put it on shirts through Printful or Printify. Order one sample of your own shirt first — if you wouldn't wear it, don't sell it. What it leads to: a merch line with no stock, no boxes piled up at home, and your designs earning while you sleep.
